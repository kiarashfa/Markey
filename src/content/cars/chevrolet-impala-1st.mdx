The Impala began life in 1958 as a dressed-up Bel Air rather than a car in
its own right: longer wheelbase, a shorter greenhouse, brushed-aluminium
trim, but no separate "Bel Air" badging to distinguish it in showroom
literature.[@wikipedia-chevrolet-impala] It rode on a new X-frame chassis
GM claimed was more torsionally rigid than the perimeter frame it replaced,
though the design proved worse in side-impact protection than the
construction it superseded, a trade Chevrolet made for two model years before
moving on.

This entry covers only the 1958 model year, GM's 50th-anniversary year, in
which the Impala was built solely as a two-door hardtop or convertible; the
four-door and wagon body styles that came to define later Impalas did not
arrive until the redesigned second generation for 1959.
