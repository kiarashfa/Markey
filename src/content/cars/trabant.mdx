East Germany's answer to the people's car: a small, front-wheel-drive saloon
with a two-stroke twin and body panels made of duroplast — a hard plastic
moulded from recycled cotton waste and phenol resin — because the country had
plenty of that and not enough sheet steel.[@wikipedia-trabant] Four models
were built across 34 years on essentially one 1950s design, and by the time
production stopped in 1991 the car itself had become a symbol of the economy
that built it, remembered as much for what never changed about it as for
what it was.
