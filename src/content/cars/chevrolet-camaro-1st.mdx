Introduced in September 1966 for the 1967 model year, the first Camaro shared
its F-body platform with the Pontiac Firebird and its subframe design with
the contemporary Chevy II Nova.[@wikipedia-chevrolet-camaro-first-generation]
Twelve different engines were available by 1969, from the standard 230-cubic-inch
straight-six up to a 427-cubic-inch COPO big-block Chevrolet never officially
listed in sales literature; the RS, SS and Z/28 option packages did the work
of turning the same basic car into anything from a boulevard cruiser to a
Trans-Am homologation special.

The Z/28's advertised 290 hp was, by Chevrolet's own later admission,
deliberately understated to keep the car under an informal one-horsepower-
per-cubic-inch limit insurers and racing bodies used for classification. This is a
case where the "official" figure is known to undersell the engine, recorded
here as the manufacturer's published rating rather than corrected upward.
