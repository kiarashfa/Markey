The first Mercedes-Benz officially sold as an S-Class, replacing the W108/109
in 1972 with a body designed by Friedrich Geiger and styling cues — the
ridged tail-lamp lenses that resist dirt build-up — borrowed from the
contemporary R107 SL.[@wikipedia-mercedes-benz-w116] It was also the first
Mercedes-Benz saloon offered with anti-lock brakes, added as an option
across the range from 1978.

At the top of the line sat the 450 SEL 6.9, introduced in 1975: at 6.8
litres the largest engine fitted to a post-war Mercedes-Benz, matched to the
full hydropneumatic self-levelling suspension that was merely optional on
the rest of the range. In North America, the W116 also introduced the 300
SD — the world's first production turbodiesel passenger car, built to help
Mercedes-Benz meet the United States' new Corporate Average Fuel Economy
rules without giving up a six-figure model in the lineup entirely.

Power and torque figures in this entry are taken from the car's first
engine series (1972-1975 for most trims); the W116's ten-year run saw two
further revisions, generally trading a little peak power for lower emissions
and better drivability, and each trim's note here says so.
