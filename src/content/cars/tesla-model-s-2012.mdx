Fourteen years of one nameplate, never formally split into generations the
way most manufacturers would: Tesla's own documentation and Wikipedia's
article both treat the Model S as a single car that was updated twice, in
2016 and 2021, rather than replaced.[@wikipedia-tesla-model-s] The three
trims recorded here are snapshots of that evolution rather than a fixed
range: the entry-level 85 of the 2012 launch car, the dual-motor P90D of
2015 that made all-wheel drive standard on performance models, and the
current tri-motor Plaid, whose 1,020 hp combined output and 1.98-second
0–60 mph time made it briefly the quickest-accelerating production car sold
anywhere.

That 1.98-second figure is deliberately not converted into a 0–100 km/h time
here: a 0–60 mph run and a 0–100 km/h run are different quantities measured
over different distances, not the same number in two units, and no
directly-measured 0–100 km/h figure is published for the Plaid. Its 200 mph
top speed, by contrast, is a straightforward speed conversion and is stored
as 322 km/h.
