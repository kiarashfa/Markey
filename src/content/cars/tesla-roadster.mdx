Tesla's first car, and the first highway-legal series-production vehicle to
use lithium-ion battery cells, the Roadster proved a small California startup
could sell a genuinely quick electric sports car years before any established
manufacturer tried.[@wikipedia-tesla-roadster-first-generation] Built on a
modified Lotus Elise chassis — glass and body panels came from France,
chassis assembly was in England, and final assembly was in California — it
sold around 2,450 units before Tesla replaced it with the Model S platform.

A single generation, sold as the base 1.5/2.0/2.5 and, from 2010, the more
powerful 2.5 Sport.
