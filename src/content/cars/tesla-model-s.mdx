Tesla's first car developed in-house rather than adapted from a Lotus
chassis, on sale from 2012 and, at the time of writing, scheduled to end
production in 2026 without ever having been given a second
generation.[@wikipedia-tesla-model-s] The engineers who designed it began by
buying a Mercedes-Benz CLS and cutting a Roadster battery pack into its
floor, before settling on the CLS's proportions as a starting point for an
entirely new car. Markey covers it as a single long-running generation, in
line with Wikipedia's own treatment: one article, two major mid-life updates
(2016 and the 2021 "Palladium" refresh that introduced the tri-motor Plaid),
and no clean generational break to split it at.
