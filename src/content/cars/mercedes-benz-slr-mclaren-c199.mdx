Grew out of the 1999 Vision SLR concept, itself a tribute to the 300 SLR
"Uhlenhaut Coupé" racer of 1955, and reached production only after
Mercedes-Benz brought in its Formula One partner McLaren to engineer and
build it.[@wikipedia-mercedes-benz-slr-mclaren] Every car was assembled at
McLaren's technology centre in Woking, England, not at a Mercedes-Benz
plant. That was an unusual arrangement for a car wearing the three-pointed star.

The hand-built, supercharged 5.4-litre V8 stayed in production essentially
unchanged for the run's whole seven years, growing from 626 PS in the
original coupé to 650 PS in the 722 Edition of 2006 (named for Stirling
Moss's start number in the 1955 Mille Miglia) and finishing with the
windscreen-less Stirling Moss speedster of 2009, some 200 kg lighter than
the standard coupé.

The standard coupé's own 0-100 km/h time is not recorded here. The only
figure Wikipedia's article publishes for it is Car and Driver's independently
measured 0-60 mph time of 3.4 seconds, a different quantity, not converted
to a 0-100 km/h figure in this entry. The Roadster, 722 Edition and Stirling
Moss variants do have manufacturer-published 0-100 km/h figures, and those
are used as given.
