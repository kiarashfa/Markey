Unveiled in August 2009 and built from October 2010 at Nissan's Oppama
plant, the original Leaf grew out of three in-house prototypes (the
Cube-based EV-01 and EV-02, and the Tiida-based EV-11) into the first
electric car sold at real volume rather than leased to fleets, as Nissan's
earlier Altra and Hypermini had been.[@wikipedia-nissan-leaf-first-generation]
US and UK production followed from 2013, at Smyrna, Tennessee and
Sunderland respectively.

Its range is the clearest case in this entry of a manufacturer's own figure
diverging from an independent regulator's: Nissan quoted 160 km on the NEDC
cycle, while the EPA's own five-cycle test, the more rigorous of the two,
rated it at 117 km, a gap wide enough that the US Federal Trade Commission
publicly challenged the EPA's own number as still too generous. This entry
records the EPA figure, not Nissan's, as the independently regulated
measurement.
