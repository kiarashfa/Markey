Underneath the reworked body sits a Fiat 124 that was changed in roughly 800
places for Soviet conditions: raised ground clearance, thicker steel, drum
rear brakes changed to a Soviet self-adjusting design, a stronger clutch, and
a completely new overhead-camshaft engine designed by NAMI that Fiat itself
never used.[@wikipedia-vaz-2101] Under the licensing deal, AvtoVAZ could not
sell the car in Italy or compete against Fiat in other Western European
markets it was already selling in, which is part of why the exported car
was rebadged as a Lada rather than sold as a Fiat.

The manufacturer's own specification table gives this car's output under
three different standards (Soviet GOST, European DIN, and American SAE)
for the same engine, and they do not agree with each other (46, 44 and 48 kW
respectively for the base 1.2). That is not a conflict to resolve; each is a
real, differently-defined figure for the same physical engine, and DIN is
recorded here as the most useful basis for comparing this car with everything
else in the catalogue. The larger 21011 engine's specification table gives no
DIN figure at all, and the article's prose separately quotes "67 hp" for it
without saying whether that means metric PS or mechanical horsepower; rather
than guess, this entry uses the table's directly-stated GOST figure instead.
