The first Supra shared its doors and rear section with the contemporary
Celica liftback, but its front panels were stretched 129.5 mm to fit an
inline-six where the Celica had a four-cylinder engine. The car was sold in Japan as the
Celica XX, and everywhere else as the Celica Supra.[@wikipedia-toyota-supra]

The two trims recorded here bracket the generation's engine range: the launch
Japanese-market MA45 with the smallest 2.0-litre six, and the final-year MA47
with the largest 2.8-litre unit sold worldwide, including North America. Both
carry the generation's weight range rather than an engine-specific figure
(the infobox gives only one 1,150-1,270 kg span for the whole generation), so
the lighter and heavier ends are assigned to the smallest and largest engines
respectively, a reasonable mapping rather than a manufacturer-confirmed
per-engine weight.
