The Enzo's two published weights (1,480 kg kerb and 1,255 kg dry) sit 225 kg
apart, which is a normal gap for a car with a small fuel tank and no fluids in
the lighter figure, not a conflict between sources. The kerb figure is what is
stored here, consistent with how this site treats every other car's mass.

Top speed is recorded as the independently tested 355 km/h rather than a
single lower factory claim, because the article gives no separate
manufacturer figure to prefer over it. Unlike some entries on this site,
there is no lower "official" number being overridden here, just one measured
result.[@wikipedia-ferrari-enzo]

Acceleration is a genuine gap. The article gives 0-60 mph and 0-100 mph times
but no 0-100 km/h figure, and the two imperial figures are not the same
quantity as the metric one this site stores, so `zeroToHundredKph` is
omitted rather than estimated from a mismatched unit.
