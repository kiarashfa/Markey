Mercedes-Benz's smallest car and its first attempt at a premium supermini,
launched in 1997 as a short, tall five-door hatchback built around a novel
"sandwich floor" that pushed the engine and running gear beneath the cabin
rather than in front of it.[@wikipedia-mercedes-benz-a-class] Four
generations have followed, moving the car from B-segment supermini to
C-segment hatchback along the way, and finally adding a saloon body in 2018.

This entry covers the original W168 of 1997 — a car more famous for failing
a Swedish elk-avoidance test than for anything in its spec sheet, and
recalled and re-engineered as a result — and the third-generation W176 of
2012, the first A-Class properly aimed at hot-hatch buyers via the A 45 AMG.
The second (W169) and fourth (W177) generations are gaps in this catalogue.
