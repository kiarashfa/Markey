Like the Murciélago and Gallardo that followed it, the Diablo was never
formally split into generations by Lamborghini; it ran for eleven years on
one platform, revised repeatedly, and this entry follows that structure by
treating each major revision as a trim rather than inventing generation
boundaries the car itself never had.[@wikipedia-lamborghini-diablo]

The clearest documented split is drivetrain: the base 1990 Diablo was
rear-wheel drive only, and the 1993 VT added a viscous-coupled all-wheel-drive
system that could send up to 25% of torque forward. Both made the same
492 PS/362 kW at launch (the VT's difference was traction, not output), and
Wikipedia's own specification table gives each drivetrain a different
front/rear weight split (41/59 rear-drive, 43/57 all-wheel drive), which is
recorded here rather than assumed.

The 1999 facelift replaced the pop-up headlamps with fixed units borrowed
under licence from the Nissan 300ZX, added variable valve timing, and lifted
the SV to 530 PS/390 kW. The final VT 6.0 stroked the block to six litres; the
article states the stroke change (80 mm to 84 mm) but not the resulting
displacement directly, so that figure is recorded as `estimated` rather than
`verified`, with the basis stated in its own note.
