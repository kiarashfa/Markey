The second-generation Q7 moved onto the MLB evo platform it shares with the
Bentley Bentayga, Lamborghini Urus and Porsche Cayenne, and added a genuine
plug-in hybrid line — both a diesel-electric e-tron TDI and, later, petrol
TFSI e variants.[@wikipedia-audi-q7] Wikipedia's own powertrain table for this
generation is unusually complete, giving power, torque and 0-100 km/h times
for fourteen distinct engine and hybrid combinations sold across the run.

As with the first generation, only one weight range is published for the
whole line-up — 1,910-2,445 kg — rather than a figure per engine. The lightest
mainstream diesel and the heaviest plug-in hybrid recorded here take the two
ends of that range, on the same reasoning as the first generation's entry:
plausible, not manufacturer-confirmed for those exact trims, and flagged as
such in each trim's own note.

The 60 TFSI e's power and torque are Audi's own published combined system
figures for the turbocharged V6 plus electric motor, not a sum performed by
this entry.
