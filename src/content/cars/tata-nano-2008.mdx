**The Nano never had a second generation**, so this entry spans its whole
production run, 2008 to 2018.[@wikipedia-tata-nano] The car barely changed
mechanically — the same 624 cc rear-mounted twin powered it from launch to
withdrawal — but the 2015 GenX facelift added a rear hatch that actually
opened (the original car's rearmost panel didn't open at all; the boot was
reachable only by folding the back seats) and offered a 5-speed automated
manual alongside the standard 4-speed. Wikipedia treats the whole run as one
article rather than splitting out GenX as its own generation, and this entry
follows that: GenX is recorded here as a trim.

Two figures needed particular care. The petrol engine's 38 PS converts
cleanly to kW, but the CNG bi-fuel version's rating is published only as "33
horsepower," with nothing to say whether that means mechanical or metric
horsepower — it is converted here as 33 PS, on the strength of every other
figure for this engine being metric, and the field's note says so rather than
presenting it as settled. And the article's own published fuel-economy line —
"25.35 kilometres per litre (4.24 L/100 km)" — doesn't actually convert:
25.35 km/L is 3.94 L/100 km, not 4.24. That looks like an error already
present in the source rather than two figures in real conflict, and the entry
keeps the direct conversion of the stated rate while recording the mismatch
rather than picking a number silently.

Being a car sold almost entirely in one market, the usual US and Canadian
cross-checks — EPA, NHTSA's Canadian Vehicle Specifications, the Dutch RDW
register — return nothing for it, which is why weight distribution is absent
here rather than filled in with a generic rear-engine layout average, and
frontal area is estimated rather than measured.
