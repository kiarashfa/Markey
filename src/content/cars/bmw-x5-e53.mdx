Developed just after BMW's acquisition of Land Rover, the E53 shares its
hill-descent control and off-road engine management with the contemporary
Range Rover L322, and its engines and electronics with the E39 5
Series.[@wikipedia-bmw-x5-e53] BMW deliberately sent 62% of engine torque to
the rear axle even in all-wheel-drive form, choosing to feel like one of its
own sedans rather than a conventional 4x4 — a decision every X5 since has
kept.

The source's own engine table states output only as "hp" without saying
whether that means metric PS or mechanical horsepower, common practice in a
German-market article but still an ambiguity this site does not resolve
silently — both trims below convert on the metric-PS reading with the
alternative noted alongside.
