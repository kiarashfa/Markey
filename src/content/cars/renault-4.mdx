Two cars share this name, thirty-one years apart, with nothing mechanical
in common: the original 4 (or "4L"), an over eight million-selling hatchback
built from 1961 to 1994 on a torsion-bar chassis derived from the
4CV, and the Renault 4 E-Tech, a battery-electric crossover revived from the
name alone in 2025.[@wikipedia-renault-4][@wikipedia-renault-4-e-tech] This
catalogue treats them as two generations of one nameplate, in keeping with
how Renault itself markets the new car as a direct successor, even though
no part of the original survives in it.
