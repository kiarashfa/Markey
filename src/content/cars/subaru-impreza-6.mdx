Revealed at the 2022 LA Auto Show, the sixth Impreza dropped both the sedan
body style and the manual gearbox, leaving a five-door hatchback with a
Lineartronic CVT as the only configuration sold.[@wikipedia-subaru-impreza]
Subaru made the car more of a driver's tool than before regardless: standard
EyeSight driver assistance, the dual-pinion steering rack borrowed from the
WRX, and ten percent more torsional rigidity from increased use of
high-strength steel. The 2.5 RS badge returned after an 18-year absence,
this time on a naturally aspirated 2.5-litre rather than a turbocharged one.

Two trims are recorded here: the base/Sport 2.0-litre and the 2.5 RS. Both
horsepower figures are published only as US-market mechanical horsepower, so
the conversions carry no reading ambiguity. The source's own infobox states
a "Manual" kerb weight for this generation despite its own prose confirming
the manual gearbox was discontinued; only the automatic (CVT) figure is
recorded here, since it is the only real specification of a car Subaru
actually sells.
