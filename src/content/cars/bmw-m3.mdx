The performance flagship of BMW's 3 Series, built by BMW's in-house
motorsport division since 1986 and produced for every 3 Series generation
since.[@wikipedia-bmw-m3] The first M3 existed for one reason: Group A
touring car homologation demanded 5,000 road cars, so BMW built them, and
the model line the rule required has outlived the rule itself by four
decades.

This entry covers the original E30 of 1986 (a four-cylinder homologation
special that never officially reached six figures of annual production and
was never meant to) and its six-cylinder successor, the E36 of 1992, the
first M3 sold as a saloon and a convertible as well as a coupé. Every
generation after the E36 (E46, E9X, F80, G80 and beyond) is a gap in this
catalogue, not an omission from the car's history.
