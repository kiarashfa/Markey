The original 911, built for twenty-five years without a full replacement
(what changed underneath it was engine size, fuel injection and, from 1974,
US-mandated impact bumpers, not the basic recipe of a flat-six hung out
behind the rear axle).[@wikipedia-porsche-911-classic] It is also the
generation that gave the 911 line its Targa roof, introduced in 1966 as an
open-topped body that could still meet the US rollover rules a full
convertible could not.

The two trims recorded here sit at opposite ends of the range. The base 2.0
of 1964 made 130 PS from an air-cooled 2.0-litre flat-six; weight and torque
figures for that earliest car are not reliably sourced in the article body
and are left blank rather than borrowed from a later, heavier model year. The
930 Turbo of 1978, sold in North America simply as the Porsche Turbo, added
forced induction and an intercooler for 300 PS. It is a car remembered as much for
its turbo lag and demanding handling as for being, at the time, the fastest
production Porsche sold.
