The third Twingo, revealed at the 2014 Geneva Motor Show, abandoned its
predecessors' front-engine layout for a rear-mounted engine driving the rear
wheels. This was a change made possible by "Project Edison", a shared platform
developed jointly with Daimler for this car and the third-generation Smart
Fortwo and Forfour.[@wikipedia-renault-twingo] Moving the engine let Renault
give the car a five-door body, a tighter 8.6 m turning circle from its 45°
steering angle, and more cabin space, at the cost of boot capacity. The GT
model, added in 2016, was the range-topping petrol turbo.

In 2020 the range gained the Twingo Electric (badged Z.E., or E-Tech
Électrique in France), sharing its rear-mounted motor and much of its
drivetrain with the Smart EQ Forfour. It is recorded here as a fourth trim on
this generation rather than as its own entry, matching how the source treats
it, a stopgap electric city car built on the outgoing platform rather than a
new generation, sold alongside the two petrol engines until the whole range
ended production in 2024.
