Launched at the 1995 Frankfurt Motor Show as a replacement for the Renault
19, the first Mégane carried over its predecessor's floorpan, engines and
transmissions under new, more rounded bodywork designed by Michel Jardin
under Patrick Le Quément.[@wikipedia-renault-megane] It introduced the
"bird beak" front grille that would define Renault's styling language for
years, and it made safety a selling point in a way few C-segment cars had
before: a pillar-mounted three-point belt for the rear middle seat, standard
front pretensioners and load limiters, and a driver's airbag were unusual
specification for 1995. A Scénic-badged compact MPV derivative, launched a
year later, was successful enough to become the standalone Renault Scénic.

Three trims are recorded here, spanning the 1.6, 2.0 and 1.9 dCi engines. The
source's own spec table gives every horsepower figure as plain "hp" with no
PS or mechanical-horsepower qualifier — an ambiguity this catalogue resolves
by reading it as PS, the near-universal European convention of the period,
while recording in each figure's note how much a mechanical-horsepower
reading would move it. No torque, boot volume or kerb weight is published for
these engines in the source.
