Launched in September 2013 after a concept-car preview in 2011, the i3 was
built around what BMW called LifeDrive: an aluminium "Drive" chassis
carrying the battery, motor and crash structure, topped by a carbon-fibre
"Life" passenger cell.[@wikipedia-bmw-i3-hatchback] The construction let
BMW keep the car light despite the battery, and gave it a shape unlike
anything else BMW sold: rear-hinged doors with no B-pillar.

Three trims are recorded here, spanning the car's battery upgrades and its
one genuinely different powertrain option. The base i3 used a 125 kW rear
motor and an 18.2 kWh usable battery at launch; the optional REx variant
added a 647 cc two-cylinder engine, borrowed from a BMW maxi-scooter and
derated to meet emissions rules, that charged the battery but never drove
the wheels; its own power output is recorded on the engine block, but it is
never added to the motor's 125 kW, because the two do not combine into one
crankshaft figure. The sportier i3s, added for 2018 on a larger 120 Ah
battery, is recorded at its highest published output; the ordinary i3 on
the same battery made slightly less.

The REx's all-electric range (72 miles/115.87 km, before the generator
starts) and its total range with the generator running (150 miles/241.40
km) are two different quantities. This entry stores the former as the
electric range and states the latter only in a note, rather than
conflating a battery-only figure with a combined one.
