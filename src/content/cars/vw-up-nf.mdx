Designed by a team under Klaus Bischoff, the Up launched in late 2011 as
Volkswagen's answer to the city-car class it had largely ceded since the
original Lupo, sharing its NSF platform with the Škoda Citigo and SEAT Mii
built alongside it in Bratislava.[@wikipedia-volkswagen-up] Volkswagen kept
adding to the range for over a decade: a CNG-capable Eco Up, a jacked-up
Cross Up, and, in December 2016, the Up GTI, a proper hot hatch on a car
that size, using a high-pressure turbo version of the same 999 cc
three-cylinder engine and the only Up ever sold with a six-speed manual.

An electric e-up! arrived alongside the launch range and was substantially
revised in 2019 with a larger 36.8 kWh gross battery pack (32.3 kWh usable)
in place of the original 18.7 kWh unit, extending its range by roughly 60%.
Four trims are recorded here: the base 1.0 MPI, the mainstream 1.0 TSI, the
Up GTI, and the second-generation e-up!. Every power figure in the source's
own tables is already given directly in kW, so no PS conversion or reading
ambiguity applies to any of them, unlike several other cars in this
catalogue from the same era.
