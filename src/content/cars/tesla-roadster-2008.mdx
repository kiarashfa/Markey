The article's own performance claims for the Roadster are given only in
imperial units (0-60 mph in 3.7 or 3.9 seconds depending on model, a 125 mph
top speed), and only the top speed is stored here, converted like-for-like
from mph to km/h. The 0-60 mph acceleration time is not the same quantity as
the 0-100 km/h figure this site stores for every other car, so it is left
empty rather than treated as an equivalent number.[@wikipedia-tesla-roadster-first-generation]

Torque is a genuinely different kind of figure from a combustion car's: the
Roadster's 200 lb-ft (271 N·m) is available immediately and stays constant
from 0 to 6,000 rpm, rather than peaking at one engine speed. The article is
explicit about this being characteristic of the electric motor rather than an
unusual spec for this particular car.

Mass, range and battery capacity are recorded identically for both trims:
the article gives one weight and one EPA range figure for the whole
generation, with no separate number published for the higher-output 2.5
Sport specifically, and the note on that trim says so.
