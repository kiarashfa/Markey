Launched in 2018 as a ground-up redesign that deliberately kept the old
W463 chassis code (Mercedes has never explained why, beyond declining to
confirm the W464 designation several outlets guessed at).[@wikipedia-mercedes-benz-g-class]
Almost nothing carried over from the outgoing car except the headlamp
washers, the push-button door handles and the spare-wheel cover; underneath,
the recirculating-ball steering gave way to a rack-and-pinion system, solid
front axle to independent suspension, and the body grew in every dimension
while somehow weighing about 170 kg less.

The two figures worth reading carefully here both come from US-market
press figures given only in horsepower and pound-feet, not kW or N⋅m: the
G 63's 577 hp and 627 lb-ft, and the base G 500's 416 hp and 450 lb-ft. Both
are converted directly from the imperial figure with the conversion factor
stated on the field; neither the G 63's claimed 0–60 mph time nor its 149 mph
top speed is treated as a 0–100 km/h figure, and the acceleration claim is
omitted rather than guessed at.
