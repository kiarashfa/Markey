Mercedes-Benz's first compact car, and a genuine engineering departure: a
tall, short five-door body over a "sandwich floor" that put the engine and
transmission on an angled sub-frame beneath the passenger cell, chosen to
maximise cabin space and, in theory, to let the whole unit slide harmlessly
under the floor in a severe frontal impact.[@wikipedia-mercedes-benz-a-class]

Weeks after launch in 1997, a Swedish magazine rolled a pre-production A-Class
during the "moose test", a sharp lane-change manoeuvre, and published the
footage. Mercedes-Benz first denied the car had a problem, then recalled
every unit sold (2,600 cars) and suspended sales for three months while it
added electronic stability control and revised the suspension, a fix that
cost more than a tenth of the car's original DM 2.5 billion development
budget. ESC was optional on most cars in 1997; the A-Class recall is
frequently credited with pushing it toward becoming standard equipment
across the industry.
