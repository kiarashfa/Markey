Designed by Michel Boué in his own time, outside his regular duties at
Renault, and rushed into a formal programme once executives saw what he had
drawn, the 5 launched in January 1972 with a steeply raked hatchback that
still looked modern next to the Mini and Hillman Imp it competed
against.[@wikipedia-renault-5] Its motorsport offshoot, the Renault 5
Turbo, took the name and general shape but moved the engine amidships and
drove the rear wheels. It is a completely different car under the skin, built to
homologate a Group 4 rally car, and recorded here as a trim only because
Wikipedia's own article treats it as part of the 5 family.

Five trims are recorded, from the original 845 cc entry engine up to the
mid-engined Turbo homologation special. Every horsepower figure in the
source is given in PS and converted here at the standard metric rate; kerb
weight is only published as a range across the ordinary front-engined cars,
so the lowest figure is recorded at generation level rather than assigned
to a specific trim.
