Unveiled at the 2007 Tokyo Motor Show after seven years of development, the
R35 replaced the Skyline GT-R's four-wheel steering with a rear-mounted
transaxle version of the ATTESA E-TS all-wheel-drive system, aiming for
near-even 54:46 front/rear weight distribution.[@wikipedia-nissan-gt-r] Every
VR38DETT engine was hand-built by one of a small number of named "Takumi"
craftsmen at Nissan's Yokohama plant for the car's entire run — a detail
Nissan used in marketing throughout, and one of the few things that did not
change across seven distinct model updates.

Output rose in five discrete steps over 18 years — 480, 485, 530, 550 and
finally 570 PS — each tied to a specific facelift and engine revision, with
the Nismo variant, introduced in 2014, always sitting above the standard car
at 600 PS. Three trims are recorded here: the original 2007 launch car, the
final 2016-2025 standard car, and the Nismo, rather than all seven
intermediate steps the source documents.
