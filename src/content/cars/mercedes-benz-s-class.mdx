Mercedes-Benz's flagship saloon and the car the rest of the range is built
underneath. The nameplate is older than the badge: large Mercedes-Benz
saloons date to 1954, but "S-Class" ("Sonderklasse", special class) was
first officially applied in 1972, to the car this catalogue starts
with.[@wikipedia-mercedes-benz-s-class]

This entry covers the W116 of 1972 (the first car to carry the name, and
the debut of the 450 SEL 6.9, a 6.8-litre halo saloon with full
hydropneumatic suspension) and its successor, the W126 of 1979, widely
regarded as the generation that defined the modern S-Class formula of
understated bodywork over class-leading engineering. Every generation since
the W126 is a gap in this catalogue, not an omission from the car's history.
