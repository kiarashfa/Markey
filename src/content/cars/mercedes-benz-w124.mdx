Sold from 1984 as the 200 through 300E, and only renamed "E-Class" for its
final two years on sale, the W124 is the generation most often cited as the
high-water mark of old-school Mercedes over-engineering — a reputation built
on cars that racked up enormous mileages as taxis, and a body shape with a
drag coefficient of 0.28 that was remarkable for a three-box saloon of
1984.[@wikipedia-mercedes-benz-w124]

Its most unlikely variant is recorded here alongside a mainstream 300E: the
500E, developed and partly assembled by Porsche because its widened,
handbuilt fenders would not fit down Mercedes' own W124 production
line.[@wikipedia-mercedes-benz-500-e] Porsche built the chassis, engineered
the suspension, and shuttled unfinished cars back and forth between its
Zuffenhausen plant and Mercedes' Sindelfingen factory for final assembly —
an arrangement that produced barely 10,000 cars but a car the contemporary
press nicknamed "the wolf in sheep's clothing," for looking, at a glance,
like any other W124.
