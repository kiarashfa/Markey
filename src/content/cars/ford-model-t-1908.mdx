**The Model T never had a second generation**, in the sense every later car in
this catalogue does. Ford's stated philosophy was to get the design right once
and then hold it still: the same 20 hp, 2.9-litre engine and the same
planetary transmission were fitted from the first car in October 1908 to the
last in May 1927.[@wikipedia-ford-model-t] What changed underneath the
constant mechanicals was the body (a dozen-plus styles across nineteen
years) and, above all, the price. This entry spans the whole run for the same
reason the Citroën 2CV's does: Wikipedia treats it as one article, and the
evolution belongs in the trims rather than in a second hub entry.

The two trims recorded here, the open **Runabout** and the touring-bodied
**Touring**, are the cheapest and the best-known of the dozen-plus body styles
respectively, and together they carry the Model T's real story, which is a
price collapse. The Runabout fell from $825 in 1909 to $260 in 1925, a 68%
nominal drop while the car itself barely changed; the Touring fell from $850
to $290 over the same years. The 1913 moving assembly line at Highland Park is
what made that possible, and it is arguably a bigger part of this car's
history than any of its body styles. The assembly list above is a sample of
the roughly seventy plants the infobox names on five continents, not the
whole list.

A few figures could not be sourced and are left out rather than guessed. No
seat count is given for either body style in the article, so none is recorded
here: "Runabout" and "Touring" conventionally imply two and five seats, but
that is a naming convention, not a cited figure. No drag coefficient exists
for a car built before aerodynamics was a design discipline. And because the
Model T's 42 mph top speed never reaches 100 km/h, a 0–100 km/h figure would
not mean anything and is not recorded. This is the same reasoning the 2CV's earliest
trim uses.

The taxonomy has no "phaeton" or "tourer" body-style tag, so the open,
folding-top Touring is filed here as `convertible`, a reasonable but
imperfect fit; a period Touring car had side curtains, not wind-up glass.
