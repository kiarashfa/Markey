The third and current generation, unveiled at the 2016 Paris Motor Show with
a new "cascading grille" that has since defined Hyundai's whole
range.[@wikipedia-hyundai-i30] It comes in more body styles than any i30
before it: hatchback, estate ("Wagon") and a fastback liftback. Once
again, it wears an entirely different name in North America, sold there as the
Elantra GT.

Two petrol engines are recorded here, a 1.0-litre three-cylinder and a
1.4-litre four-cylinder turbo; the performance-focused i30 N and its
substantially different chassis and outputs are left for a future pass
rather than folded in as another trim of this one. Dimensions and kerb
weight are both published as ranges spanning body style and trim (kerb
weight even spans up to the 275 PS N model), so the lowest figure in each
range is recorded at generation level, not assigned to a specific trim.
