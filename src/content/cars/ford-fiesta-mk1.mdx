Ford's first small front-wheel-drive car, styled by Tom Tjaarda and built at
four plants across three countries from launch. That was a scale of commitment that
signalled how seriously Ford took the new European supermini
class.[@wikipedia-ford-fiesta-first-generation] It went on to be the ninth
best-selling car in Britain in its first year and third by 1981, before the
Austin Metro and a wave of new rivals ate into that lead.

Wikipedia's own performance table for this car gives 0-60 mph times and mph
top speeds, not 0-100 km/h or km/h; this entry deliberately leaves those
schema fields empty rather than convert between the two. A 0-60 mph time is
not the same measurement as 0-100 km/h, and treating them as interchangeable
is exactly the kind of error this site is built to avoid.
