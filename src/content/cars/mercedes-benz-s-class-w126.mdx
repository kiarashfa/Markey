The second S-Class, on sale from December 1979 and, for many buyers of the
period, the car that made the badge synonymous with engineering rigour
rather than mere size.[@wikipedia-mercedes-benz-w126] It ran for nearly
twelve years with only one major revision, in 1985-86, and introduced a
pillarless coupé body (C126, badged SEC) alongside the saloon for the first
time in the S-Class's history.

The range spanned a 280 SE straight-six to a 5.5-litre V8 in the 560 SEL,
and the same 560 SEL badge covers several genuinely different power outputs
depending on market and emissions equipment: a European "ECE" car without a
catalytic converter made 220 kW, while catalyst-equipped "RÜF" and "CAT"
versions sold in the same years made as little as 178 kW. This entry records
the non-catalyst European figure and says so, rather than picking whichever
number happened to be highest.

A separately published 0-60 mph time for the US-market catalyst car is not
converted to a 0-100 km/h figure here; the two describe different speed
thresholds, and the entry uses only the figure that was actually measured to
100 km/h.
