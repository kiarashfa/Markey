The FD3S is the RX-7 most people picture: a rounded, low, pop-up-headlight
coupé launched in October 1991 around the 13B-REW, the first mass-produced
sequential twin-turbo engine exported from Japan.[@wikipedia-mazda-rx-7] Mazda
sold it in North America for only four model years, 1993 to 1995, before weak
demand and a strong yen ended US imports; production continued in Japan,
progressively lighter and more powerful in its final Series 8 Type RS form,
until 2002.

Japanese-market output was effectively capped at 280 PS across the industry's
self-imposed 206 kW (280 PS) gentlemen's agreement, which is why the car's
peak figures barely moved across a decade of otherwise continuous
development; later cars got lighter and better cooled instead of
outright more powerful. The limited-edition "Spirit R" of 2000–2002, the very
last cars built, is not broken out as its own trim here because the source
does not give it a separate power figure from the Type RS it was based on.
