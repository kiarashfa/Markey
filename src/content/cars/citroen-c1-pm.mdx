The original C1, launched in 2005 as one of three near-identical cars built
side by side at a joint-venture factory in the Czech Republic — the others
being the Peugeot 107 and the Toyota Aygo, whose engine the C1 also
uses.[@wikipedia-citroen-c1] It became an unlikely fixture of amateur
endurance racing, cheap and simple enough to run for 24 hours the way owners
once raced the 2CV.

Fuel economy for both engines is a genuine self-contradiction in the
source, not a disagreement between two references: the article's own body
text gives one figure (marked "citation needed" in the underlying wikitext)
while its engine specification table gives a different, more specific one
for the same engine. Both are recorded in the note; the table figure, being
the more precisely attributed one, is the value stored.
