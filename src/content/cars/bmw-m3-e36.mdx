The E36 replaced the E30's four-cylinder homologation engine with a straight
six (the BMW S50) and, for the first time, put the M3 badge on a saloon
and a convertible as well as the coupe it launched with.[@wikipedia-bmw-m3]
It reached the United States only in 1995, three years after the rest of
the world, and in a deliberately detuned form: American cars used the
lower-output S50B30US and later S52B32 engines throughout the run, kept
below the European car's output to hold the price down.

A 1995 facelift enlarged the engine from 3.0 to 3.2 litres, added a sixth
manual gear, and introduced BMW's first automated-manual "SMG" transmission
outside the United States. The one trim-specific kerb weight this catalogue
found (1,515 kg, for a 1996 European coupe) comes from a period sales
brochure rather than a road test, and is recorded only against the
facelifted European trim it actually describes.

Three market/facelift combinations are recorded as separate trims here
because their power, torque and displacement figures genuinely differ; a
European car and a US car sold under the same "M3" badge in the same years
are not the same engine.
