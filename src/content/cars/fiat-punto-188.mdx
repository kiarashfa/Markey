An all-new body and chassis in 1999 that kept the outgoing Punto's basic
silhouette, with a new torsion-beam rear axle and, for the first time on
this car, a Fiat Wish hardtop-convertible concept shown alongside it that
never reached production.[@wikipedia-fiat-punto] It stayed in production for
eleven years, well past the 2005 launch of its own successor, the Grande
Punto, selling in parallel as the cut-price "Punto Classic" until 2010.

Wikipedia's engine table for this generation is unusually complete: metric
top speed, a 0-100 km/h time and a combined consumption figure are given
directly for every engine, none of them converted from an imperial source, a
rare case in this catalogue where nothing needed reconstructing from a US or
mixed-unit original.
