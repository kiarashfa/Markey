Revealed at the 1998 Geneva Motor Show and developed under Richard
Parry-Jones, the first Focus was engineered around a class-leading
independent rear suspension — Control Blade — and a tall, computer-modelled
interior that made the car feel bigger inside than its footprint
suggested.[@wikipedia-ford-focus-first-generation] Ford had originally
planned to keep the Escort name for this generation, and only switched at
the last minute; a German court briefly forced Ford to avoid the word
"Focus" there entirely, after a magazine publisher claimed prior rights to
it.

Three trims are recorded here: the 1.6 Zetec-S/SE petrol, the 1.8 TDCi
diesel, and the RS — the first Focus RS, front-wheel drive with a Quaife
limited-slip differential to manage its 215 PS turbocharged four. Two of
the three engine displacements in this entry (the 1.6 petrol and 1.8
diesel) are recorded as `estimated`: the article states only the rounded
litre figure for each, so the commonly documented exact cubic-centimetre
displacement is marked as an estimate rather than presented as a sourced
fact.
