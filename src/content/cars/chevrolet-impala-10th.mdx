The final Impala, launched for 2014 on GM's extended Epsilon II platform
shared with the Cadillac XTS and Buick LaCrosse, and the first full-size
North American sedan in twenty years to earn Consumer Reports' top rating,
95 out of 100.[@wikipedia-chevrolet-impala] Three engines were offered across
its run: a 2.5-litre four as the volume choice, a 3.6-litre V6 at the top,
and a 2.4-litre four with belt-alternator-starter mild-hybrid assistance
("eAssist") in between, dropped along with the plain 2.5 in the car's final
2020 model year, which was sold with the V6 only.

A CNG bi-fuel version, built on the 3.6, was also offered to fleet and retail
buyers from 2015 (briefly the only factory-built full-size CNG car sold in
North America) but is not broken out as its own trim here since the source
gives it only a combined driving range, not a separate power or economy
figure of its own.
