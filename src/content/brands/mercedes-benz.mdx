The name joins two men who never met: Karl Benz, who patented the Benz
Patent-Motorwagen in 1886 and is generally credited with the first practical
automobile, and Gottlieb Daimler, whose separate engine company licensed the
name "Mercedes" from a customer's daughter in 1902.[@wikipedia-mercedes-benz]
Daimler and Benz's companies merged in 1926, under economic pressure common to
the German motor industry of the period, to form Daimler-Benz and the
combined Mercedes-Benz marque. The three-pointed star predates the merger —
it was a Daimler mark, said to represent motorisation on land, sea and air —
and the laurel wreath around it was Benz's. Mercedes-Benz has spent the
century since as one of the industry's default reference points for engineering
conservatism: real innovations (the crumple zone, ABS, the airbag) delivered
without much appetite for changing what a Mercedes saloon is expected to look
or feel like.
