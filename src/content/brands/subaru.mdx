Subaru traces its lineage to Nakajima Aircraft Company, a Japanese
wartime aircraft manufacturer broken up by the post-war Allied occupation;
several of its successor companies merged in 1953 to form Fuji Heavy
Industries, which built its first car (the Subaru 1500) the following
year and took the Subaru name for the brand.[@wikipedia-subaru] The
company itself was renamed Subaru Corporation in 2017. It has built its
identity on symmetrical all-wheel drive and horizontally opposed "boxer"
engines across nearly its entire passenger-car range, a combination few
other manufacturers have committed to as consistently.
