Ferdinand Porsche's engineering consultancy designed cars for other
manufacturers — including, controversially, the Volkswagen Beetle — for
decades before it built one under its own name.[@wikipedia-porsche-ag] The
Porsche 64 of 1939, a streamlined Beetle-based racer, is usually cited as the
first car to wear the family name; series production of Porsche's own models
began after the war, in Gmünd, Austria, with the 356. The rear-engined
air-cooled layout that car established carried through to the 911, launched
in 1963, and the company has spent every decade since living with and
occasionally departing from that inheritance. Volkswagen Group has held a
controlling stake since 2012, closing a loop that started with Ferdinand
Porsche's own prewar work for the company.
